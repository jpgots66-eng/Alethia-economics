The Mechanism
What Signal DX actually is — and why it is built differently
Signal DX is a deterministic diagnostic. A founder answers 20 structured questions. The system reads the pattern in those answers and surfaces what is actually constraining growth — not a generic assessment, but a specific read on this business against 13 known failure mechanisms.
The scoring engine is deterministic. The framework is fixed. The questions were designed by Travis Roderick from 30 years of operator experience across the specific failure modes that founder-led businesses hit at scale.
The Framework
Model-first, not data-first
Signal DX starts with 13 known paths — the finite set of mechanisms that actually constrain growth. Every question is designed to eliminate possibilities, not gather more information.
The Output
Root mechanism, not plausible narrative
The result is a causal finding checked against founder reality — not "most likely" with uncertainty remaining, but a specific mechanism ready to verify in a live conversation.
The Archetype System
Compound patterns, not stacked findings
5 primary archetypes and 3 fallback archetypes cover every known combination. None fires unless the exact question combination is present. Two constraints appearing together are often one underlying pattern.
The Standard
Seen, heard, understood — never let off the hook
Every finding is grounded in behavior, not adjectives. No jargon, no hedging, no language that lets a founder read a real finding and decide it does not apply to them.
The Structural Gap
Where dedicated AI tools fail — and why more data does not close the gap
AI-powered diagnostic tools gather publicly available data — filings, reviews, market signals, leadership profiles — and synthesize it at speed. They identify that something is structurally wrong. That is a real capability. It is not the same capability as Signal DX.
Public signal is the downstream exhaust of organizational dysfunction. By the time a constraint surfaces in a Glassdoor review or a margin trend, the organization has been living with that constraint for years. An AI tool that reads public data perfectly tells you that something is wrong. The gap — the one that determines whether the intervention works — is the why.
1
Signal vs. Root Cause
AI tools read what is publicly visible. Signal DX reads what is structurally true. A CEO with 20 years of tenure reads as stability from the outside. In three of our documented engagements, long tenure was the binding constraint — centralized control that had prevented any independent operating capability from developing. The public data found the right signal. It could not read it correctly.
2
Framework vs. Accumulation
AI tools accumulate data and surface patterns. Signal DX narrows against a fixed, validated set of 13 known growth constraints. The value is not speed of analysis. The value is the prior. Every question was designed to eliminate a specific failure mode. More data does not improve on that. It produces more noise around the same signal.
3
Interpretation vs. Output
The most dangerous output an AI diagnostic can produce is a confident misread. Across every engagement in our comparison set, the public data correctly identified that something was structurally wrong. In every case, the mechanism underneath — the actual root cause — required internal access and operator interpretation to surface. A diagnostic tool that produces a confident answer without internal access is producing a liability, not a diagnosis.
"The question is not whether AI tools are useful. The question is what they are useful for. They surface that something is wrong. Signal DX is built for the harder problem: determining why — with enough precision that the intervention holds."
